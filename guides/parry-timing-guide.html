Parry Timing Guide

Frame-by-frame breakdown of parry windows, perfect parry mechanics, and how to build the muscle memory for consistent counters because honestly parrying is what separates people who beat the game from people who actually get good at it.

Home / Guides / Parry Timing Guide

Parrying is the highest-skill mechanic in Phantom Blade Zero and I'm not gonna sugarcoat it you either learn this or you hit a wall around the mid-game and stay there forever, a successful parry staggers the enemy generates Sha-Chi and opens a damage window but a failed parry leaves you completely vulnerable and in some fights one missed parry means you're dead which is honestly kind of brutal but also what makes the combat so satisfying when you get it right, this guide breaks down the exact frame data timing windows and training methods you need to go from inconsistent to automatic and tbh I've probably spent more time in the training arena than actually playing the game and that's not an exaggeration

But anyway. Let's start with the basics.

The Three Parry Tiers

📈

Parry Quality Tiers

Tier 1: Block (Early Parry)

Pressing parry too early results in a standard block and honestly I've done this more times than I can count, you take reduced damage and no Sha-Chi is lost but the enemy is not staggered and no Sha-Chi is generated which basically means you survived but got nothing out of it, in Phantom Blade Zero blocking is safe but produces no offensive benefit you're trading damage reduction for resource generation and that's fine when you're panicking but it's not how you win fights tbh

Tier 2: Normal Parry

Pressing parry within the active window of roughly 8 frames before the attack lands triggers a normal parry and this is your bread and butter honestly, you take no damage the enemy is staggered briefly and you generate 10-15% Sha-Chi which is pretty good all things considered, this is the reliable parry the one you should be aiming for most of the time it creates a small punish window enough for 2-3 light attacks or one heavy attack so it's worth going for every time you see blue

Tier 3: Perfect Parry

Pressing parry within 3 frames of impact triggers a Perfect Parry and ngl when this happens it feels incredible the screen flashes white time slows for 1.5 seconds the enemy is staggered for an extended duration and you generate 20% Sha-Chi plus drain 15% from the enemy's bar which is huge, this is the parry you should aim for on every Brutal Move even though you won't hit it every time and that's okay nobody hits these 100% not even speedrunners I don't think

Frame Data Summary: Normal Parry Window = 8 frames (133ms at 60fps). Perfect Parry Window = 3 frames (50ms). The difference is about the duration of a single eyeblink and yeah that's not much. Practice until the 3-frame window becomes muscle memory because it literally transforms boss fights from impossible to manageable.

Brutal vs Killer: Read the Glow

Phantom Blade Zero color-codes every enemy attack and if you don't learn to read these colors you're gonna have a bad time and I mean really bad like dying to the tutorial boss bad, identifying the attack type in the wind-up phase is the first step to a successful counter and here is how to read each color because once your eyes learn this pattern the game opens up completely:

ColorAttack TypeCounterWindowPunish
Blue FlashBrutal MoveParry8 frames (normal) / 3 frames (perfect)3-4 light attacks or 1 heavy
Red FlashKiller MoveGhoststep10 frames (normal) / 5 frames (perfect)2-3 light attacks from behind
Purple FlashUnblockablePositioning dodgeVariable (15-20 frames to react)None, avoid and reset

The key to reading these at speed is not to look at the color directly but to look at the enemy's weapon and honestly this one tip changed everything for me, Brutal Moves always have a blue glow on the weapon during wind-up and Killer Moves have a red glow, once you train your eyes to check the weapon glow rather than the screen flash your reaction time will improve by like 100-200ms which doesn't sound like much but in this game that's the difference between a perfect parry and eating the full combo and dying so yeah it matters a lot

So remember. Weapon not flash.

Attack Timing Profiles

Every boss attack follows a predictable timing arc and once you understand the arc you can start anticipating when to press parry instead of just reacting which is way more consistent, here's how different attack speeds work and what your brain should be doing during each phase:

Fast Attacks (200-400ms wind-up)

Found on dagger-wielders and swift bosses and these are the ones that feel unfair at first because the wind-up is so short that you basically have to parry reactively there's no time to read and decide, for fast attacks train a reflex response as soon as you see the weapon move press parry even if it turns out to be a Killer Move that requires Ghoststep the parry input will at least result in a block that mitigates damage which is better than eating the full hit honestly and I've found that just accepting you'll block sometimes instead of perfect parrying every time makes these fights way less stressful

Wind-up 200ms Parry Window 133ms Perfect 50ms Recovery 300ms

Medium Attacks (400-800ms wind-up)

The most common attack type and honestly the most fun to parry because you have enough time to identify the color and choose Parry or Ghoststep without feeling rushed, focus on reading the weapon glow during the first 200ms of the wind-up then execute the appropriate counter and after a while this becomes automatic you won't even think about it you'll just do it and that's the goal

Wind-up 600ms Parry Window 133ms Perfect 50ms Recovery 400ms

Slow Attacks (800ms+ wind-up)

Telegraphed heavy attacks and weirdly these are the easiest to parry but the most punishing to miss because they hit like a truck, the key is not to parry too early and this is where most people mess up they panic-button and parry at the 600ms mark instead of waiting for the actual strike, count one-one-thousand in your head after the wind-up starts then parry and I know that sounds like silly advice but it actually works you'd be surprised how many people press parry as soon as they see the wind-up and then get demolished

Wind-up 1000ms Parry Window 133ms Perfect 50ms Recovery 600ms
Slow Attack Rule: When a boss raises a weapon overhead and pauses, wait for the downward movement to begin. Do not parry during the pause or you will miss and probably die. The actual strike comes 200-300ms after the pause ends and that is your parry window right there.

Anyway. Next up is Ghoststep.

Ghoststep Timing

Ghoststep is the counter to Killer Moves and tbh it's the most stylish mechanic in the game and I love it so much, it functions like a dodge that phases through attacks but the Ghoststep window is slightly more forgiving than parry at 10 frames normal and 5 frames perfect, the positioning requirement is stricter though you must Ghoststep directly toward the attack not sideways or away and this goes against every survival instinct you have which is why it takes so long to learn

Perfect Ghoststep at the 5-frame window slows time for 1 second and places you directly behind the enemy and this is the most powerful defensive option in the game no question, it fully negates damage repositions you for a back-attack and generates 25% Sha-Chi all at once, landing a Perfect Ghoststep against a boss's Killer Move is often the turning point of a fight like you can literally feel the momentum shift and it's so good you'll chase that feeling forever once you experience it

Ghoststep Drill: Go to the training arena and fight the basic soldier enemy. Only use Ghoststep, no parrying no attacking no anything else. Practice Ghoststepping through every attack until you can do it without looking at your character and I promise this sounds boring but it works. When you can consistently land Perfect Ghoststeps you are ready for any boss in the game.

Training Drills

Drill 1: The Parry-Only Run

Equip no weapons fists only and fight the first training enemy you can only press parry no attacks no Ghoststep no movement just parry, win by parrying every attack until the enemy tires out and this drill removes all complexity and forces pure parry timing, recommended time is about 5 minutes daily and tbh I still do this as a warmup before serious boss attempts it's just a good habit to build

Drill 2: Color Callout

Fight any boss and say the attack color out loud before you counter so like blue parry red Ghoststep purple dodge just literally say it out loud, speaking forces your brain to process the visual information before acting and it feels ridiculous at first but after 3-4 boss attempts with this drill the identification becomes subconscious and you'll stop having to think about it which is the whole point

Drill 3: Perfect Parry Challenge

Equip Venomous Softblade for the extended parry frames and fight an early boss like Ogre Hammer Lord, your goal is to land 10 Perfect Parries before the fight ends and you count them out loud because that makes you focus, if the fight ends without reaching 10 restart and try again and you'll be amazed how fast the 3-frame timing becomes natural after a few rounds of this

Drill 4: Ghoststep Only

Fight Snake Sovereign using only Ghoststep counters no parrying no blocking and honestly this is painful at first because Snake Sovereign doesn't mess around, since it has several Killer Moves this is the ideal training boss for Ghoststep, focus on the red flash and practice the toward-the-attack direction and after about ten deaths you'll start to get it and then it feels amazing I promise

Weapon-Specific Parry Windows

Different weapons have different parry frame windows and this is one of those things the game never tells you that makes a huge difference, understanding these differences helps you choose the right weapon for your parry skill level and here's the data:

WeaponNormal Parry FramesPerfect Parry FramesNotes
Venomous Softblade12 frames (200ms)5 frames (83ms)Best for learning parry timing
White Python and Red Viper8 frames (133ms)3 frames (50ms)Standard parry window
Jagged Steel8 frames (133ms)3 frames (50ms)Standard window, bleed bonus
Celestial Lance6 frames (100ms)2 frames (33ms)Tightest window, highest parry reward
Savage Axe10 frames (167ms)4 frames (67ms)Forgiving window, slow recovery
Phoenix Wing8 frames (133ms)3 frames (50ms)Standard window, fire on parry
Building up: Start with Venomous Softblade (12-frame window) to build confidence because that extra 4 frames makes a massive difference when you're learning. Once you consistently land Perfect Parries with it switch to a standard 8-frame weapon. Then finally challenge yourself with Celestial Lance's 6-frame window. Each step down tightens your timing and by the time you're done you'll be parrying everything.

Common Parry Mistakes

Button mashing parry is probably the biggest reason people can't get consistent and I did this for like the first twenty hours of playing so don't feel bad about it, pressing parry multiple times in rapid succession resets the parry window each time so if you miss the first press pressing again immediately does not help it actually delays your next opportunity, one press per attack that's the rule and breaking the button mashing habit is genuinely harder than learning the timing itself

Parrying too early is the most common mistake on slow attacks and it's such a natural instinct to press the button when you see the wind-up but you have to wait for the weapon to begin moving toward you not the wind-up and I know I said this already but it bears repeating because literally everyone does this wrong at first including me, also confusing Brutal and Killer is a death sentence parrying a Killer Move results in a full hit and Ghoststepping a Brutal Move wastes positioning, train the color identification until it's automatic and not respecting recovery after a missed parry is how you die twice, you cannot act for 300-400ms after a whiffed parry and in that window the enemy can land a full combo so if you miss do not try to parry again immediately just dodge away and reset and take a breath and try again you get the idea
