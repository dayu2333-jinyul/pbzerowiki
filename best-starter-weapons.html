5 Best Starter Weapons in Phantom Blade Zero

Your first weapon choice shapes the entire early game. These five are the most forgiving, the easiest to upgrade early, and the most effective before you unlock advanced options. No spoilers past the first two zones.

Home / Weapons / Best Starter Weapons

Look, I've watched too many people grab the flashiest weapon on the selection screen and then spend three hours dying to the first elite. Phantom Blade Zero doesn't hold your hand with weapon descriptions at the start, and some of those early options are traps — they look cool but have steep learning curves or require upgrade materials you won't see for hours.

Here are the five weapons that actually work in the opening zones, ranked from safest to most aggressive. All of these can be acquired before the second boss.

1

Jagged Steel (Sword)

SwordBalancedBest for beginners

This is the weapon I tell everyone to start with. It's the first sword you find, sitting in the training grounds of the prologue area. You literally cannot miss it.

  • Damage: Medium. Won't melt bosses but won't leave you slapping enemies for five minutes either.
  • Speed: Fast enough to sneak hits between enemy combos. Recovery frames are forgiving — you can whiff an attack and still have time to parry.
  • Sha-Chi generation: Solid. Three-hit light combo fills about 1.5 bars, which means you can use your first Phantom Edge every 12-15 seconds of active combat.
  • Upgrade path: Evolution materials for swords are the most common in the first two zones. You can hit +2 before the third boss without any grinding.
Best for: First-time players who want to learn the Sha-Chi rhythm without fighting their own weapon. The parry windows on sword moves feel slightly wider than other weapon types — whether that's intentional design or just good animation readability, I'm not sure, but it helps.
2

Ironfang Spear (Spear)

SpearLong ReachSafe playstyle

Found in the Abandoned Garrison, about 20 minutes in. If you keep dying to the armored soldiers in the courtyard, switch to this.

  • Range advantage: You can poke enemies from outside their melee range. Against the early-game shield soldiers — who are a nightmare for sword users — you can chip them down without ever entering their attack zone.
  • Thrust combo: The heavy thrust has a slight lunge that closes distance. It feels awkward for the first ten minutes but becomes second nature.
  • Weakness: Low damage per hit. Boss fights drag on. You're trading speed kills for safety, and against some of the later early-game bosses that's a worthwhile trade.
  • Upgrade materials: Spear evolution stones are rarer than sword stones. Plan to hit +1 around the third boss, +2 around the fifth.
Best for: Players who want to study enemy patterns from a safe distance. If you're coming from Elden Ring and loved the Naginata playstyle, this will click immediately.
3

Venomous Softblade (Sword)

SwordStatus EffectAggressive

This is the first "elemental" weapon most players find. It drops from the Serpent Cultist mini-boss in the Whispering Grotto, and it applies a poison DoT on every third consecutive hit.

  • Poison mechanic: Three hits builds one poison stack, capping at five stacks. At five stacks the enemy takes damage equal to roughly one light attack every two seconds for ten seconds. Against early bosses with large health pools, this adds up fast.
  • Trade-off: Base damage is lower than Jagged Steel. If you can't maintain pressure, you're doing less damage overall. This weapon rewards nonstop aggression.
  • Sha-Chi synergy: Poison ticks count as "landed attacks" for Sha-Chi generation. This means your bar fills even during dodge windows. It's borderline broken against slow bosses.
Best for: Aggressive players who never let off the attack button. If you find yourself backing off to heal a lot, stick with Jagged Steel — the Softblade's lower base damage will punish passive play.
4

Thunderclap Dual Blades (Dual Blades)

Dual BladesFastHigh skill ceiling

Purchased from the wandering merchant in the Crossroads Bazaar for 3,000 spirit coins. Fastest attack speed in the early game, but the damage-per-hit is the lowest of any starter option.

  • Sha-Chi machine: The six-hit light combo generates almost two full bars. You'll be firing off Phantom Edges and Killer Moves more often than any other weapon type. If you love special moves, this is your weapon.
  • Parry risk: The long combo strings mean you're committed to attack animations for longer. You can't cancel out of the fourth, fifth, or sixth hits. Against enemies with fast counter-attacks, this gets you killed.
  • Boss performance: Mixed. Melts bosses with large punish windows, struggles against aggressive bosses that don't give you time for full combos.
Best for: Players with action game experience (DMC, Bayonetta) who are comfortable with long combo commitments. Not recommended if this is your first action RPG.
5

Bone Crusher (Greatsword)

GreatswordHeavyHigh risk

I'm putting this at number five with a warning: this weapon will kill you more than enemies will. It's slow, every swing is a commitment, and the recovery time is brutal. But when it works, it really works.

  • Damage: Highest per-hit of any early weapon. Charged heavy attacks can one-shot basic enemies and chunk bosses for 15-20% of their health bar.
  • Stagger: The real reason to use this. Heavy swings stagger most early-game elites in two hits. You can chain-stagger certain enemies to death without them ever getting an attack off.
  • Learning curve: You need to memorize enemy attack patterns because you can't react — you have to predict. Every swing locks you in for roughly 1.2 seconds.
  • Best pairing: Equip the Steel Thread Phantom Edge to immobilize enemies before winding up a charged heavy. This combo alone makes the Bone Crusher viable for boss fights.
Best for: Souls veterans who are comfortable with ultra-greatsword timing. If you beat Dark Souls 3 with the Fume Ultra Greatsword, you'll feel at home. Everyone else: start with Jagged Steel and come back to this on a second playthrough.

Which One Should You Pick?

If you're reading this before starting the game: pick Jagged Steel. It's boring advice, I know, but the sword moveset is the most polished, the upgrade path is the most forgiving, and you'll have the smoothest learning curve. You can switch weapons at any bell checkpoint, so you're not locked in.

If you're already past the prologue and struggling: grab the Ironfang Spear from the Garrison. The range advantage gives you breathing room to actually learn what the enemies are doing instead of panic-dodging.

If you're confident in your action game skills and want to style on enemies: Thunderclap Dual Blades or Venomous Softblade. Just be ready to eat some deaths while you learn the combo timings.
