Wan Jun "Coppermaul" — Boss Guide

Wan Jun, known as Coppermaul, is one of the two bosses showcased at Gamescom 2025 and one of the earliest major skill checks in Phantom Blade Zero. He's a gigantic humanoid boss wielding a sledgehammer roughly the size of a small car. The fight takes place in a quarry arena with destructible pillars that can be used for cover during his phase-three desperation attacks. Defeating him drops the Bashpole, a greatsword-class weapon confirmed during the Gamescom demo to drop directly from his corpse after the kill.

Coppermaul looks slow because of his size. He is not slow. His attacks have enormous wind-ups followed by sudden, devastating acceleration. The most common death in this fight is dodging during the wind-up and getting hit during the recovery frames when the hammer finally comes down. Patience kills Coppermaul. Panic gets you killed by him.

Phase One: The Hammer

Coppermaul has three basic attacks in phase one. The overhead slam: he raises the hammer above his head, holds it for what feels like an eternity, then brings it down in a vertical strike that creates a shockwave. The hammer itself is a one-hit kill on Sha-Chi Master difficulty. The shockwave does about 40% of your health and knocks you down. Dodge the hammer to the side, not backward. The shockwave travels forward in a line, so dodging sideways avoids both the hammer and the wave. The timing: wait for the hammer to start moving downward. Not during the wind-up, not when the hammer reaches its highest point. When it starts moving. Dodge then.

The horizontal sweep: Coppermaul swings the hammer in a wide arc at chest height. This is a Brutal Move (blue flash). Parry it. The parry window is generous because the sweep is slow, and a successful parry staggers him for about three seconds, the longest stagger window of any boss in the game. This is your primary damage opportunity. Every sweep he throws is free damage if you're ready to parry.

The stomp: Coppermaul raises one foot and stomps, creating a circular shockwave. This is not a Brutal or Killer Move, just a regular attack that covers a wide area around him. Dodge backward twice to clear the radius. The stomp is his fastest attack and the one that catches players who are too aggressive in melee range.

Phase Two: Dust Clouds

At 60% health, Coppermaul slams the ground and fills the arena with dust clouds that reduce visibility to about two character-lengths. This is the fight's signature mechanic. You can't see the hammer wind-ups clearly. You have to react to sound cues and partial animations through the dust.

The horizontal sweep becomes the most dangerous attack in phase two because you can see the blue flash through the dust but you can't see the hammer's position clearly enough to judge the parry timing. The solution: the sweep always comes after he takes two steps toward you. If you see him take two steps through the dust, prepare to parry.

The overhead slam gains a new property in phase two: it now creates three shockwaves instead of one, each traveling in a different direction. The safest movement in phase two is to dodge diagonally forward, past Coppermaul, which puts you behind him where no shockwaves travel. The Hybrid Flex build is the best choice for this phase because Dragon Fire charged shots let you deal damage from the edge of the dust cloud without committing to melee range where the stomp punishes you.

Phase Three: Desperation

At 25% health, Coppermaul enters desperation. His attacks gain fire damage and leave burning patches on the ground that persist for about 15 seconds. The arena shrinks as more and more of the floor becomes hazardous. The destructible pillars become critical here. Standing behind a pillar blocks the hammer but the pillar breaks after one hit. There are four pillars in the arena. That's four free hammer dodges.

In desperation, Coppermaul adds a fourth attack: a running charge that covers the entire arena. He lowers his shoulder and sprints at you. This is a Killer Move (red flash). Dodge into it for Ghoststep. The timing is more forgiving than it looks because the charge has a long active window. Dodge when he's about two character-lengths away.

The winning strategy for phase three is patience. Wait for the charge, Ghoststep it, land your 3-5 free hits, then reposition behind a pillar. Wait for the next charge. Don't chase him. Don't engage during his regular combo strings. Just wait for charges and punish them. The fight takes longer this way but you'll survive.

Drop: Bashpole (guaranteed first kill). Greatsword-class weapon with the highest stagger damage in the game. Its Power Surge, Earth Splitter, is a massive overhead slam with a shockwave that hits everything in a wide cone. The Bashpole is the best secondary weapon for aggressive builds because its charged heavy can chain-stagger bosses that are already vulnerable. Fully evolved at +4, it gains a Mastery Skill that causes shockwaves on every charged heavy, not just the Power Surge.
