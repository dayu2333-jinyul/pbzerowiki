How Hard Is Phantom Blade Zero?

Compared head-to-head with Sekiro, Elden Ring, Black Myth Wukong, Lies of P, and Nioh 2. Numbers on boss aggression, parry timing forgiveness, and combat complexity — not just gut feelings.

Home / Soulslike Difficulty Ranking

The question everyone asks: "is this harder than Sekiro?" The answer is complicated, because PB Zero's difficulty works differently from other soulslikes. It doesn't have a stamina bar, which makes it feel more forgiving in some ways. But it also gives bosses more aggressive movesets to compensate. You can't hide behind a shield and chip away — the game pushes you into a rhythm of attack-build-spend and punishes passivity.

Here's how it stacks up against the five soulslikes people compare it to most often, rated on three axes: boss aggression (how relentless enemies are), parry timing (how tight the defensive windows are), and system complexity (how much you need to learn before the combat clicks).

PB Zero vs Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice

The closest comparison — both built around parry rhythm

Sekiro is the game PB Zero gets compared to most, and it's fair. Both use a posture-breaking combat system where parrying is your primary defensive tool. But they feel different in practice.

  • Parry window: PB Zero's parry window is slightly wider than Sekiro's. It's closer to Lies of P's generous timing than Sekiro's razor-thin frames. You'll land parries more consistently here.
  • Punishment for mistakes: Sekiro is harsher. In PB Zero, a missed parry means you eat the hit and lose health. In Sekiro, a missed deflect against certain bosses is a one-shot. PB Zero gives you more chances to recover.
  • Offensive pressure: PB Zero rewards aggression more than Sekiro does. The Sha-Chi system means that sitting back and waiting for openings is literally costing you damage potential. Sekiro lets you play more reactively.

Bottom line: If you beat Sekiro, you have the mechanical foundation to handle PB Zero. The parry timing will feel generous by comparison. The adjustment is learning to stay aggressive rather than waiting for openings.

PB Zero vs Elden Ring

Different types of difficulty — Elden Ring is about options, PB Zero is about execution

Elden Ring is harder in the sense that it gives you fewer tools to deal with pressure. No Sha-Chi-powered special moves, no Ghoststep teleport dodges, no Phantom Edge slot machine. But Elden Ring is also easier in the sense that you can overlevel and summon help to trivialize any fight.

  • Build flexibility: Elden Ring wins here. You can break the game with the right build. PB Zero balances its weapons more tightly — there's no Comet Azur equivalent that deletes bosses in five seconds.
  • Boss complexity: PB Zero bosses have more complex movesets on average. Elden Ring has harder individual bosses (Malenia is still the queen), but PB Zero's average boss is mechanically denser.
  • No summons in PB Zero: Elden Ring lets you summon spirit ashes or other players for every boss. PB Zero has no multiplayer summons and very limited NPC summoning. You have to beat every boss solo.

Bottom line: If you beat Elden Ring without summons, you're ready. If you relied on spirit ashes and multiplayer to clear bosses, PB Zero will be an adjustment — you have to learn every fight yourself.

PB Zero vs Black Myth: Wukong

Wukong is more forgiving, especially in the early game

Black Myth Wukong shares some visual DNA with PB Zero — both are Unreal Engine 5 Chinese-developed action games with flashy combat. But Wukong is noticeably easier, especially at the start.

  • Healing: Wukong gives you a gourd with multiple sips from the start. PB Zero has no healing items — your health restores at bell checkpoints. This makes PB Zero's resource management more punishing in long zones.
  • Spells as crutches: Wukong's immobilize spell and pluck of many let you bypass learning certain boss patterns. PB Zero has no "skip the mechanics" button — every boss has to be learned.
  • First boss difficulty: PB Zero's first real boss is harder than anything in Wukong's first two chapters. Wukong has a gentler onboarding curve.

PB Zero vs Lies of P

Similar parry-focused combat, comparable difficulty curve

Lies of P is probably the fairest comparison in terms of overall difficulty. Both games have parry-centric combat, both punish passive play, and both have bosses with multi-phase fights that demand consistency.

  • Guard regain: Lies of P's rally system gives you health back for attacking after blocking. PB Zero doesn't have this — damage sticks. On the other hand, bell checkpoints are more frequent than Lies of P's Stargazers.
  • Weapon variety: PB Zero has more weapon types and clearer build identities. Lies of P's weapon assembly system is deeper but harder to optimize without a guide.

PB Zero vs Nioh 2

Nioh 2 is more complex; PB Zero is more punishing

Nioh 2 has a steeper learning curve because of its stance system, ki pulse mechanics, and loot management. PB Zero strips a lot of that complexity out but compensates with more aggressive enemies.

  • System complexity: Nioh 2 is harder to learn. Three stances per weapon, ki pulsing, soul core abilities, guardian spirits, yokai shift — it's a lot. PB Zero's Sha-Chi system is simpler to understand, harder to master in execution.
  • Enemy damage: PB Zero enemies hit harder relative to your health pool. In Nioh 2, you can tank multiple hits with the right armor. In PB Zero, three hits from a boss will kill you regardless of your build.

Final Difficulty Ranking

If you're trying to figure out where PB Zero lands on the soulslike difficulty spectrum, here's the rough order from hardest to easiest, based on a first playthrough on standard difficulty:

  1. Sekiro — still the king of parry difficulty. Tighter windows, harsher punishments.
  2. Nioh 2 — more complex systems to learn, but easier combat execution once you do.
  3. Phantom Blade Zero — slots right here. Demanding but fair, with enough tools to handle every situation.
  4. Lies of P — very close to PB Zero. Slightly easier bosses, slightly worse checkpoints.
  5. Elden Ring — the widest difficulty range of any soulslike. Trivial with the right build, brutal without.
  6. Black Myth: Wukong — the most accessible of the bunch. Strong spells and generous healing make the first playthrough manageable.

One last thing: PB Zero has difficulty options. You can drop to Story Mode at any time if a boss is walling you, then switch back. That alone makes it more approachable than Sekiro or Lies of P, which lock you into one difficulty. Use the flexibility — it's there for a reason.
